What should a fence contractor's work include?

A fence contractor should first clarify the job: which parts of the property are included, what kind of fence is planned, and whether the work is a new installation, a replacement, or a repair. The written scope should also say whether old fence removal, gate work, cleanup, and adjustments for sloped ground are included.

If something matters to you, such as keeping a particular tree or matching an existing gate, put it in the scope rather than relying on a conversation.

What does a local professional check at the property?

A site visit helps identify conditions that can change the plan. The contractor should look at the route, slopes, access for materials, existing posts, gates, and anything that may affect digging. They should also ask about property boundaries instead of treating an old fence as proof of the legal line.

  • Where the fence should sit, and whether you have a recent survey or other reliable boundary information.
  • Whether the ground is level, sloped, rocky, or obstructed by roots or existing structures.
  • How gates will open and whether their locations make sense for everyday use.
  • Whether utilities, drainage, trees, or nearby structures could affect post placement.
  • Which local rules, permits, or neighborhood requirements you should confirm before work begins.

A contractor can point out questions to resolve, but don't assume that a site visit settles boundary ownership or replaces approval from the appropriate local office or neighborhood association.

What changes the cost of a fence project?

The price depends on more than the total length. Material and fence style, height, gate count, site access, ground conditions, and the amount of old fencing to remove can all affect the work. A short section with difficult access or a steep grade may take more effort than a longer, clear run.

Ask each provider to show what is included and what could be an extra. Compare quotes based on the same materials and scope, including gate hardware, disposal, cleanup, and any preparation work. If the contractor finds an unexpected condition, ask for the change and its cost in writing before approving it.

How can you avoid delays and common hiring mistakes?

Timing can depend on the size of the job, crew availability, material availability, weather, site access, and whether boundary or approval questions need to be resolved first. Ask for an estimated start window and a realistic explanation of what might shift it. A firm date should not be promised until the main project details are understood.

  • Don't assume the fence line is correct because an old fence is already there.
  • Don't compare quotes that specify different materials or leave removal and cleanup unclear.
  • Don't rely on a verbal promise about gates, repairs, or added sections.
  • Don't start before checking applicable city or county requirements and any neighborhood rules.
  • Don't approve extra work until you understand the change to the scope and total cost.

Questions to ask before you hire

  • Which parts of the property and which fence sections are included in the written scope?
  • What materials, height, style, and gate details does the proposal specify?
  • Does the work include removing the old fence, hauling it away, and cleaning the site?
  • How will you handle slopes, access problems, roots, or other conditions found during the job?
  • Who should confirm the property line, and what local approvals should I check?
  • What could change the quoted scope or timing, and how will you get approval for changes?
  • Who will I contact if I have a question during the work or notice an issue afterward?

How much does it cost to get your fence cleaned?

Cleaning prices depend on the fence material, its size, how much buildup needs attention, and whether repairs are needed first. Ask cleaning providers what their service includes; don't assume fence cleaning is part of an installation or repair quote.

Whose responsibility is it to repair a fence?

Responsibility can depend on who owns the fence, where it sits, and any written agreement between neighbors or property owners. Check your property documents and local rules, and talk with the neighbor before arranging work on a shared boundary.

What does a fencer charge per hour?

There isn't one dependable hourly rate for every fence job, and contractors may quote a complete project rather than an hourly charge. Ask for a written estimate that explains the work, materials, and any conditions that could change the total.
