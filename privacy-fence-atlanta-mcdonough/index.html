What should privacy fence service include?

A privacy fence project should begin with a conversation about the fence line, height, material, gates, and how much of the yard you want screened. The installer should look at the ground and access before recommending a layout, rather than assuming the same approach will suit every property.

A written estimate or scope should make clear what work is included. Ask whether it covers taking out and hauling away an old fence, setting posts, gate hardware, cleanup, and any finish work. If those items are not included, find out what they would involve before agreeing to the project.

The plan should also explain where the fence will sit in relation to property lines, trees, slopes, and existing structures. Confirm how the contractor will handle changes if digging reveals an obstacle or the actual conditions differ from what was visible during the estimate.

What should a local professional check at your property?

A site visit is useful because the shape and condition of the yard can affect both the fence design and the work involved. In McDonough, as on any property, ask the contractor to review the specific lot instead of relying only on a photo or a rough measurement.

  • The proposed fence line, boundary information, and any corners or narrow areas that affect layout.
  • Slopes and changes in grade, which can affect how the fence follows the ground and how much privacy remains near the bottom.
  • Trees, roots, drainage paths, and existing structures that may limit post locations or access.
  • Gate locations, opening direction, and the clear space needed to use them.
  • How materials and equipment can reach the work area, including whether an existing fence or landscaping needs attention first.

Before anyone digs, confirm that the appropriate underground utility marking process has been arranged. Also check the property boundary using reliable records or a survey if you are unsure where it falls. A fence installed in the wrong place can create a costly dispute.

Which privacy fence material and layout should you compare?

Ask for options based on how you use the yard, how much screening you want, and the upkeep you are comfortable with. A privacy fence is not just a choice of panels. Its height, gaps, gate placement, and fit against uneven ground all affect how it works once installed.

  • Wood: Offers a familiar solid-screen look, with upkeep needs that depend on the product and finish. Ask how the boards will be arranged and whether staining or sealing is part of the project.
  • Vinyl: Can provide a consistent appearance with different care needs than wood. Confirm the product details, color, panel style, and how repairs would be handled if a section is damaged.
  • Composite: May be an option for homeowners comparing a wood-like appearance with other maintenance requirements. Check the specific product and what the contractor includes in the quote.
  • A mixed or partial layout: A solid section near a patio or seating area, combined with a different fence style elsewhere, may meet the privacy goal without screening every boundary.

These are general options to discuss with a contractor, not a claim that every provider carries each material. Ask to see the exact product or specifications being quoted, and consider how the design will look from both sides of the fence.

What changes the cost of a privacy fence?

There is no reliable price to give without knowing the fence length, material, height, layout, and site conditions. A contractor should base the estimate on your property and spell out what is included instead of giving a number that leaves out important work.

  • Total fence length, height, and the number and type of gates.
  • Material selection, product specifications, and finish requirements.
  • Slopes, uneven ground, roots, drainage concerns, and other obstacles along the fence line.
  • Removal and disposal of an existing fence, if needed.
  • Access for the crew and materials, along with any extra preparation the site requires.
  • Permit, HOA, or other review requirements that affect the planned design or work.

When comparing estimates, check that each one describes similar materials, fence dimensions, gate hardware, removal, cleanup, and labor. Ask how added work is approved and documented. A lower total may reflect a different scope, so compare the details before treating quotes as equivalent.

How can you plan for installation timing?

Timing depends on the job size, the contractor's schedule, material availability, site preparation, and any required approvals. Weather or unexpected conditions underground can also affect when work can start or how quickly it can be completed. Ask for an estimated start window and what could change it.

Before setting a date, find out whether the contractor needs anything from you, such as access arrangements, a decision on gate placement, or confirmation of an HOA review. Ask when the crew will remove an old fence, how the yard will be left at the end of each workday, and who will tell you about a delay.

What mistakes should you avoid before hiring?

  • Choosing a fence line based on assumption rather than checking the boundary.
  • Treating an HOA approval or permit as settled before confirming what applies to your property and design.
  • Comparing quotes that specify different materials, heights, gates, or removal work.
  • Overlooking how a slope or gap near the ground can affect privacy.
  • Leaving gate size, opening direction, or hardware undecided until installation day.
  • Accepting a verbal promise about cleanup, disposal, or changes without seeing it in the written scope.

Check current requirements with the relevant local authority and your HOA, if you have one. Rules can depend on the property and the proposed fence, so do not rely on a neighbor's fence or an older project as proof that the same design is allowed.

Questions to ask before you hire

  • Will you review the fence line and yard conditions in person before finalizing the estimate?
  • What exact material, height, layout, and gate hardware does the quote include?
  • Does the scope include removing and disposing of the existing fence and cleaning up afterward?
  • How will the fence follow the grade, and what should I expect around uneven areas?
  • Who is responsible for checking property boundaries, utility markings, permits, or HOA approval?
  • What is the expected start window, and what could affect the schedule?
  • How do you price and approve changes if the site conditions differ from the estimate?
  • Who should I contact if I have a question during installation or after the work is complete?

Clear answers help you compare contractors on the work itself, not just the total on the page. Keep the estimate and any later changes in writing so you and the installer have the same understanding.
