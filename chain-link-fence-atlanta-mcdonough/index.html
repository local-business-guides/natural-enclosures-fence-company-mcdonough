What should chain link fence service cover?

A chain link fence project should begin with a clear plan for what the fence needs to do. A yard for a dog, a play area, a boundary, and a larger utility space can call for different heights, gate locations, and layout details.

The scope should describe the fence line, materials, posts, gates, installation, and cleanup. If an old fence needs to come out, ask whether removal and hauling are included or quoted separately. A written scope makes it easier to compare estimates that may otherwise appear similar.

What will a local professional check before planning the fence?

A site visit helps a contractor understand the ground, access, and intended fence line. Slopes, trees, existing structures, and narrow access can affect how the fence is laid out and how much work installation takes.

The contractor should also ask where gates are needed and what will pass through them. Before work begins, confirm the property line rather than assuming an existing fence marks it. Ask how underground utility lines and any easements will be addressed, too.

Which chain link details should you compare?

Chain link is available in different heights, finishes, and configurations. The right choice depends on the purpose of the enclosure and the look and upkeep you prefer. Ask the contractor to identify the proposed material in writing, rather than relying on a general description.

  • Fence height: Choose based on the enclosure's purpose and any applicable local rules.
  • Finish: Ask about the available coated or uncoated options and what each means for appearance and maintenance.
  • Gates: Confirm the number, location, width, and type of each gate, including how it will latch.
  • Privacy additions: If you are considering slats or screening, ask whether they suit the proposed fence and how they may affect the project.

What affects the cost and timing?

Timing can depend on the size and complexity of the job, access to the fence line, material availability, weather, and the contractor's schedule. What mistakes should you avoid?

  • Do not assume the old fence is on the correct property line. Confirm boundaries before agreeing to a layout.
  • Do not compare estimates by the total alone. Check that the fence, gates, removal, and cleanup are described consistently.
  • Do not leave gate locations or opening direction to guesswork. Confirm them on the plan before installation.
  • Do not overlook utility lines, easements, or local requirements. Check what applies to your property before work starts.

It is also worth asking how the crew will handle uneven ground and transitions near buildings or existing fences. If those details are not clear in the proposal, ask for them to be explained before signing.

Questions to ask before you hire

  • What fence height, finish, and other materials are included in this estimate?
  • How many gates are included, and what are their locations and widths?
  • Does the price include removing and hauling away the existing fence?
  • How will you confirm the fence line and handle slopes or obstacles?
  • Who checks for utility lines, and what do I need to do before installation?
  • What local permits, rules, or approvals should I verify for this property?
  • What is the expected schedule, and how will delays be communicated?
  • What cleanup and warranty terms are included in the written agreement?

What local rules should you check?

Fence requirements can depend on the location and details of the property. Before scheduling work, ask the relevant local office or property association whether permits, height limits, setback rules, or other approvals apply. If the property is part of an association or has shared boundaries, check its requirements and any recorded easements as well.

A contractor can help identify questions to resolve, but do not assume that a standard fence plan is automatically approved for every lot. Confirm the applicable requirements before materials are ordered or the fence line is set.
