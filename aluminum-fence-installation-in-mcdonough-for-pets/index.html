What should aluminum fence installation for pets include?

A pet-ready installation starts with more than choosing a fence style. The installer should discuss your pet's size, behavior, and habits, then plan the fence line, height, picket spacing, gates, and any areas where a dog might squeeze through or try to dig.

Aluminum fencing is made with vertical pickets and open space between them, so it can keep a yard feeling open while marking a boundary. It may not contain every pet by itself. A small dog, a climber, or a determined digger may need closer attention to the gaps, bottom edge, gate latches, or a separate barrier.

What should a local installer check before starting?

A professional should walk the proposed fence line with you and check the slope, drainage, trees, existing structures, and access for installation. On a sloped yard, ask how the fence will follow the ground and whether the resulting openings beneath or between sections could let your pet out.

The installer should also discuss the property line, underground utility marking, gate locations, and any local requirements that may apply. In McDonough, rules can depend on the property and project, so confirm applicable permits or restrictions with the city or county before work begins rather than assuming a standard fence is automatically approved.

Walk through your pet's routine together. Point out spots where your dog already jumps, paws at the ground, or waits by a door, since those details can affect where a gate belongs and what needs extra attention.

Which aluminum fence details work best for your pet?

There isn't one picket layout that suits every dog. Ask the installer to compare the actual openings with your pet's size, including the chance of a puppy growing, a small dog squeezing through, or a pet pushing its head or paws between bars.

  • For a small or young pet, pay close attention to the space between pickets and the opening beneath the bottom rail.
  • For a dog that jumps, ask whether the proposed fence height and layout are appropriate for its behavior. No fence should be treated as a guarantee against climbing or jumping.
  • For a dog that digs, discuss how the bottom edge will meet the ground and whether another barrier or yard adjustment is needed.
  • For a yard with regular foot traffic, place gates where people can use them easily and ask about secure latches that household members can operate.

Ask to review the exact product and gate hardware being quoted, not just a photo of a finished fence. A clear description makes it easier to judge whether the design fits your pet and to compare proposals on the same terms.

What changes the cost and timing?

The price depends on the scope and conditions of the job, so a reliable quote should be based on a site review and a defined fence plan. Factors can include the total fence run, selected aluminum style and height, number and type of gates, slope, obstacles, access, and how much preparation or removal is needed.

Pet-specific choices can also affect the proposal, including picket spacing, gate hardware, and any added work to address digging or gaps along uneven ground. Ask which materials and labor are included, what is excluded, and how changes discovered during installation would be approved.

Timing depends on the installer’s schedule, product availability, weather, site conditions, and any required approvals or utility marking. Ask for an estimated start window and expected work duration, and have the company explain what could shift those estimates.

Which installation mistakes should you avoid?

  • Choosing a fence based only on appearance without checking the openings against your pet.
  • Assuming an open-style aluminum fence will stop a dog that climbs, jumps, or digs.
  • Leaving gate placement or latch details until after the fence line is set.
  • Ignoring slopes and low spots where the ground may leave a pet-sized opening.
  • Accepting a quote that doesn't identify the product, gates, preparation, cleanup, or exclusions.

Before the crew starts, walk the plan and gate locations with the installer. After installation, inspect the fence line and gates with the contractor and point out any opening or latch issue that differs from the agreed plan.

Questions to ask before you hire

  • What picket spacing and fence height are you proposing for my pet, and why?
  • How will the fence follow the slope, and what will happen at low spots?
  • What gates and latch hardware are included, and where will you place them?
  • Does the written quote include materials, labor, site preparation, and cleanup?
  • Who will complete the installation, and who should I contact if I have a concern?
  • What local approvals or utility marking should be confirmed before work starts?
  • What could change the estimated start date, duration, or final scope?
  • What warranty applies to the materials and installation?
